# 9. Grouping conventions

**Status:** accepted

## The decision

Where judging a grouping needed a rule, these were settled. They are
for the labelling guide's grouping section when it is written, and for
anyone grouping by hand.

- **A shorthand that stands for a place or its government is that
  place.** A capital used for the government ("talks with Riverside" when
  Riverside is a capital) is the country; an airport code used for the
  city ("Arrive RVS", "Flight RVS–NTH") is the city. The capital as a
  city, or the airport as a building, is its own place.
- **A person is never merged into their organisation,** even when the
  organisation bears their name ("Maria Lopez" and "Lopez for Senate"
  are two entities).
- **A sub-region is not its region,** and a city is not a street
  address in it — unless the address is just the city's venue named a
  second way.
- **A demonym joins its country** ("British" with "Great Britain").
- **A company may take its products' names,** when the products are
  tagged as the company (defensible rather than required).
- **One address split across two spans is one place.**

## Why

Each came from a case where the LLM and a reasonable reader could
disagree, and the point of a convention is that the next judgement goes
the same way. They lean towards what replacement needs: merging a
shorthand with its place lets one fake value stand for both; merging a
person with their organisation would replace a name with a company.
